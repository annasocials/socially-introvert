The Concept Project

Independent project4 products4 content worlds
The setup

Anyone can say they’re good at marketing. I’d rather you watched me think. So I took four small product brands and worked through each one the way I would if you’d hired me.

What I did

For each one, I looked for what was being overlooked, mapped the world of interests around the product, and made the content to match: profiles, captions, a mascot, a mood.

What kept showing up

The product is rarely what people care about first. A journal is a calmer morning. A hoodie is feeling understood. A dog treat is a dog mom’s joy. Once you see that, the content has somewhere to go.

What this is, and isn’t

This is independent work, so there are no client results to report, and I’d rather say that than make numbers up. What it shows is how I think and what I’d look for in your product.

Concept Projects

Independent projects I built to show how I think, not client work, but the same process I use on every product: find what's being overlooked, build the content world around it, then actually make the pieces.

The question each one answers: if I handed Anna my product, what would she see that I don't?

01
Concept Project · Digital Wellness

moonveilelysium

The product

Digital gratitude journals and printable wellness tools for people who want softer, more mindful days.

What I noticed

Nobody searches "digital gratitude journal" when they actually need one, but everyone is already looking for the feeling right before that moment: overwhelmed, running on empty, craving a reset.

The opportunity

Sell the reset, not the PDF. Meet people inside the small mindful moments they're already having, so the journal shows up already wanted instead of cold.

The bigger content world

Gentle mornings · slow living · tiny daily rituals · gratitude prompts · evening wind-downs · sunset reflection

What I created

A content system built entirely around small pauses (Gentle Reset, 3 Mindful Moments, Slow Down and Enjoy, Begin Your Day With Intentions), each piece doubling as a soft entry point into the free 7-Day Gratitude Sample.

02
Concept Project · Apparel

badmeow.co

The product

An "anti-social uniform" apparel brand: hoodies and tees for people who don't like people, powered by caffeine and corporate fatigue.

What I noticed

The product is a t-shirt, but what people are actually buying is the feeling of being understood: the specific exhaustion of small talk, back-to-back meetings, and Mondays.

The opportunity

Don't market it as clothing. Market it as a mood people already relate to, and let a recurring mascot carry the joke so the brand becomes something people want to quote and share, not just wear.

The bigger content world

Corporate sarcasm · quiet quitting humor · coffee-before-people culture · "professionally unavailable" · WFH boundaries

What I created

A meme-first content identity, "for people who don't like people," pairing product shots with deadpan captions and a recurring cat mascot so the brand reads as a personality, not a product line.

03
Concept Project · Luxury Accessories

Glossy & Glam

The product

Designer-inspired bags, shoes and accessories, shipping worldwide from Dubai.

What I noticed

In a crowded "affordable luxury" space, most competitors just post product photos. Nothing in the feed says why the piece matters to someone's actual life.

The opportunity

Sell intentional luxury, a lifestyle of quiet elegance and considered spending, and give the product an aspirational world to live in instead of a plain product shot.

The bigger content world

Quiet luxury · capsule wardrobes · gifting culture · luxury minimalism · "our happy besties" social proof

What I created

A moodboard-driven content system (Intentional Luxury, Glam & Love, Luxury Minimalists), positioning each piece as part of an elevated daily ritual, plus a recurring "our bag personality" series to boost engagement.

04
Concept Project · Pet Products

pupsandsuchtreats

The product

All-natural, handbaked dog treats, custom hound birthday cakes and paw balms, handcrafted in Pittsburgh, PA.

What I noticed

The product is food for dogs, but the real driver is the owner's love for their dog: a treat photo alone doesn't sell that, but a dog's genuine reaction does.

The opportunity

Build the content world around "dog mom" culture instead of the treats themselves: the joy of spoiling your dog, the guilt-free good-dog moment, gifting a birthday cake to a pet.

The bigger content world

Dog mom starter packs · "good dog" humor · picky-eater approval · breed-specific content · pup-approved reviews

What I created

A content system that leans on real dog reactions and owner testimonials, "my picky eater loves these," over product photography, framed as a lovable, review-driven brand people trust before they buy.
